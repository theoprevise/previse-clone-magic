START WITH THE PROPERTY

What is a DSCR loan?

A debt service coverage ratio loan is designed for investment properties. Many residential programs focus on the property’s rental income rather than using personal income and traditional debt-to-income calculations.

01

Calculate coverage

For many residential programs, qualifying monthly rent is divided by principal, interest, property taxes, insurance and association dues—often called PITIA.

02

Understand the ratio

A 1.00x ratio means qualifying rent equals the modeled PITIA payment. At 1.20x, rent is 20% higher. Neither figure alone tells you the property’s full profit.

03

Review the whole deal

Lenders also evaluate credit, reserves, down payment or equity, property, rent documentation and other program requirements. Rules and pricing differ.

A SMARTER START

Colorado DSCR questions, answered.

Good financing starts with a clear picture of the property and your plans.

What DSCR do I need?

There is no single industry-wide minimum. Some programs require 1.00x or higher; others may allow a lower ratio or no-ratio structure. A lower ratio can affect pricing, leverage and other requirements.

How much down payment is required?

It depends on the lender, credit, ratio, property, loan size and other factors. Use the calculator to explore an assumed LTV, then discuss a specific scenario with Previse.

Can I use a DSCR loan for an Airbnb?

Some lenders accept short-term rentals and may use historical income, a market-rent analysis or other documentation. Colorado municipalities and communities have different rental rules; verify permits, HOA restrictions and lender eligibility for the specific property.

Can a first-time investor or an LLC qualify?

Some programs accept first-time investors or loans vested in an eligible entity. Credit, guarantees, entity documentation and experience requirements vary by lender.

Does 1.00x mean the property is profitable?

No. PITIA generally excludes vacancy, repairs, management, utilities, capital expenses and income taxes. Include those costs in your own investment analysis.

What should I have ready?

Bring the property address, purchase price or estimated value, expected rent or lease, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, your estimated credit profile and available funds. For a refinance, include the current loan balance.
